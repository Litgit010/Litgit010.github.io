05 / Extreme gravity

A boundary we can see around an object we cannot.

Black holes emit no light of their own. Astronomers infer them through their effects on matter, light, and spacetime—and can now resolve the bright material immediately around some event horizons.

Object

M87*

Distance

About 55 million light-years

Mass

About 6.5 billion Suns

What the image means

Radio observation,
not a photograph.

The Event Horizon Telescope combined radio observatories around Earth into a virtual Earth-sized telescope. The dark center is the black hole's shadow; the luminous ring is emission from hot material bent by intense gravity.

Event horizon

The boundary beyond which nothing—not even light—can return.

Accretion disk

Hot, orbiting material that can radiate intensely before crossing the horizon.

Singularity

A prediction of general relativity where current theory reaches its limits; not directly observed.

Two broad scales

Stellar-mass black holes form from stars; supermassive black holes occupy galactic centers.

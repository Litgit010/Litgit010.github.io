04 / Stellar endings

The violent chemistry of creation.

A supernova is the catastrophic explosion of a star. It briefly becomes extraordinarily luminous and drives newly forged elements into interstellar space.

Observation / Messier 1

The Crab Nebula

Light from this stellar explosion reached Earth in 1054. The expanding remnant still surrounds a rapidly rotating neutron star—the Crab Pulsar.

About 6,500 light-years away · Supernova remnant

Phase / 01

Type Ia

A white dwarf in a binary system undergoes a runaway thermonuclear explosion. Their consistent luminosity makes them important tools for measuring cosmic distances.

Phase / 02

Core collapse

A massive star exhausts fuel in its core. Gravity wins in seconds; outer layers rebound from the collapsing core and are expelled into space.

Phase / 03

Elements dispersed

Explosions and the processes around them create and distribute many heavy elements. The calcium in bones and iron in blood trace a history of stellar evolution.

Phase / 04

What remains

Depending on the original star, the compact remnant may be a neutron star or black hole, surrounded by an expanding, energized cloud of debris.
